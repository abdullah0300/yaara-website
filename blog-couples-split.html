Fair vs. Equal: Splitting Household Expenses Proportionally by Income

By Yaara Relationship Finance Team · 7 min read · Published Oct 24, 2026
Proportional Math Guide ✓

The 30-Second Solution

Equal ≠ Fair: When partners earn different salaries, an inflexible 50/50 split drains the lower earner’s discretionary savings while the higher earner continues accumulating wealth. Shifting to an income-proportional split (e.g. 60/40 or 70/30) for joint household costs keeps both partners feeling respected, valued, and equally invested in their shared home.

When two people move in together, dividing everything straight down the middle feels like the modern, progressive choice. But when one partner earns PKR 250,000 and the other earns PKR 120,000, paying PKR 60,000 each for rent means the lower earner spends 50% of their entire salary on housing alone, leaving zero room for personal savings or self-care.

Couples Finance Research: According to relationship psychology studies, over 62% of cohabiting couples report money as their top relationship stressor. Couples who adopt proportional splits report 40% fewer arguments over shared living expenses than couples who enforce rigid 50/50 contributions.

The Proportional Split Formula

Calculating your household ratio takes less than two minutes:

  1. Add Both Monthly Incomes: Partner A (PKR 180,000) + Partner B (PKR 120,000) = PKR 300,000 total.
  2. Find Individual Percentages:
    • Partner A: 180,000 / 300,000 = 60%
    • Partner B: 120,000 / 300,000 = 40%
  3. Apply Ratio to Joint Costs: If monthly rent + utilities + groceries total PKR 80,000:
    • Partner A contributes 60% = PKR 48,000
    • Partner B contributes 40% = PKR 32,000

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a proportional split for couples?

A proportional split divides shared living costs based on each partner's percentage of total household income. For example, if Partner A earns 60% of total income and Partner B earns 40%, shared rent and utilities are split 60/40 so both retain proportional savings.
