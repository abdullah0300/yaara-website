BEFORE THE NEXT PURCHASE

A small routine.
A clearer working day.

Yaara gives your shared expenses a place. A simple agreement and a regular review make those records easier to use together.

  1. 01

    Define what you share.

    Agree which purchases belong in the group: materials, printing, deliveries, transport or shared workspace bills. Keep costs for one person’s own work outside shared expenses unless you both agree otherwise.

  2. 02

    Record the purchase with context.

    Add the amount, who paid and the shares agreed for that expense in Yaara. Keep a receipt where it helps explain the purchase, and use a separate group for a separate plan when useful.

  3. 03

    Review costs and money returned.

    Choose a time to check the shared records together. Add missing purchases, clarify anything unclear and record repayments made outside the app before deciding what remains.

How can business partners use Yaara for shared expenses?

Create a Yaara group for the purchases you agree to share. Add each expense with its amount, payer, participants and agreed shares. Keep useful receipts with the expense and record repayments made outside the app, including part payments.

What shared business costs can we record?

Examples include materials, printing, packaging, deliveries, transport for a shared job and workspace bills. Include only costs the relevant partners agreed to share. Each expense can have its own participants and shares.

What if one partner pays more of the bills upfront?

Record who paid each purchase and each partner’s agreed share in Yaara. The amount paid upfront and the share of the cost are different details. Keeping both, along with repayments, helps partners understand what remains between them.

Does every expense need to be fifty-fifty?

No. Yaara supports equal, exact, percentage, weighted and item-based shares. Agree the split for each expense and record it accordingly. The split of a purchase does not define business ownership or profit distribution.

Can we keep costs for different projects separate?

Yes. Use separate Yaara groups for different projects or plans when that makes the records easier to follow. Within each group, choose who shares each expense and the agreed amounts.

Can we attach a receipt to a shared purchase?

Yes. Keep the receipt with the expense in Yaara when it helps explain the amount or items purchased. Record the payer and agreed shares alongside it so the purchase has the relevant context.

Can a partner repay part of what they owe?

Yes. Record the repayment after it happens, including part payments. If PKR 1,000 is due and PKR 400 is returned, PKR 600 remains. Record money returned separately from new shared purchases.

Does Yaara send payments between business partners?

No. Partners pay each other through their usual payment method. Yaara records shared costs, money lent and repayments; it does not move the money.

Does an expense share represent ownership or invested capital?

No. An expense share is the portion of a purchase a person agreed to cover. This page describes shared expenses and repayments between partners, not capital contributions, ownership percentages or profit distributions.

Do all partners need connected accounts to see records?

You can keep personal records for a contact without a connected account. For another partner to access shared records, they need the appropriate connection or group access.
