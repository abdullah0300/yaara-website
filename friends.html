THE PLACES CHANGE. THE LITTLE MOMENTS DON’T.

Good friends.
Familiar questions.

Shared expenses are costs two or more friends agree to share: a meal, a ride, project supplies or a gift. The challenge is keeping the payer, participants and each person’s share clear when plans overlap.

Consumer Research: According to Bankrate consumer lending studies, 46% of adults who lent money to friends or family never got fully repaid, and 37% lost money. Yaara keeps personal loans and shared restaurant bills clear from day one, so friendships stay intact.

01 / AFTER CLASS

“I’ll get lunch.
What’s everyone’s share?”

School canteen snacks, college lunch or chai after a university lecture: one friend pays, then everyone heads to the next class. Later, the amount and who joined can be hard to remember.

A place for the bill.

In Yaara, record who paid, include the friends who joined and add their agreed shares. Keep the receipt with the expense when it helps.

For example: a PKR 1,800 lunch split equally by three friends is PKR 600 each. If you paid, the other two each owe you PKR 600 before repayments. A receipt and clear shares make the bill easier to revisit.

02 / OUT FOR DINNER

“We didn’t all
order the same thing.”

Some shared a platter. Someone only had chai. An equal split may not match what your friends agreed to share.

Agree on the share, not the guess.

Use item-based, exact or percentage shares in Yaara to match the agreement for that meal. The person who paid and each person’s part stay in the same record.

Before you add the bill, agree which items were shared and how to divide service charges or other extras. Record the final agreed amounts so “I only had chai” doesn’t become a calculation everyone has to repeat.

03 / THE RIDE HOME

“That ride was
just for us two.”

Your group has four friends. Only two took the ride. Being part of the group doesn’t make every expense everyone’s responsibility.

Include the people who shared it.

Choose only the two riders as participants in Yaara. The four-person group can stay together; this expense belongs to the people who took the ride. Use the same approach for a delivery or an activity only some friends joined.

A LITTLE CLARITY GOES A LONG WAY

Keep it simple.
Keep being friends.

How to keep shared expenses clear with friends: agree the cost, record its details, then record money returned. These habits work for a quick lunch and for plans that last several weeks.

01

Say what’s shared.

Before someone pays, agree who is involved and whether the share is equal, exact, item-based or a percentage. A meal, gift and ride can each have a different agreement.

02

Add it while it’s fresh.

Keep the amount, payer, participants and shares with the expense. Attach a receipt when it explains the details.

03

Remember the money back.

After a friend sends money outside Yaara, record the repayment, including part payments. Agree when to review the group so small amounts don’t depend on a long chat history.

Can Yaara help with school, college or university project costs?

Yes. Create a group for the classmates sharing the costs, then record materials, printing and other agreed purchases separately. Include the relevant contributors for each expense, keep useful receipts and record money paid back. Yaara tracks the shared costs; project tasks stay with the tools your team already uses.

How do we share a birthday gift when some friends also buy the cake?

Keep the gift and cake as separate expenses in the birthday group. Choose the contributors and agreed shares for each purchase. Someone can share the gift without contributing to the cake, and repayments can be recorded as they come back.

What is the difference between a shared expense and lending a friend money?

A shared expense divides a purchase among the people who agreed to share it. A personal loan records money you lent to one person to return. In Yaara, keep the loan separate from group bills and record repayments, including part payments, against it.

How can friends keep track of shared expenses?

Record each expense with the amount, who paid, who shared it and their agreed shares. Record repayments when they happen. Yaara keeps shared costs and repayments together so lunch, dinner and other plans don’t rely on one friend’s memory.

Can I create different groups for different friends?

Yes. Use Yaara groups for different circles or a specific plan, such as college friends, a birthday gift or a weekend away. Choose the participants for each expense within the group.

How do we split a restaurant bill when orders are different?

Agree on each person’s share, then record item-based, exact or percentage shares in Yaara. An equal split is also available when everyone agrees it fits the bill.

What if a friend skips a meal or a ride?

Include only the people sharing that particular expense. Someone can belong to your friend group without sharing the cost of every activity.

Can I record a small loan to a friend?

Yes. Keep personal loans separate from shared bills. Record the amount lent and any repayments, including partial repayments, to see what remains.

Does everyone need a connected account?

You can keep personal records for contacts without connected accounts. For another person to access shared records, they need the appropriate connection or group access.

Does Yaara send or collect the money?

No. Yaara keeps the record of expenses, money lent and repayments. Pay each other through the method you normally use, then record the payment back.
